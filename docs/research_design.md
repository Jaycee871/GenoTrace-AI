# Research Design

## Working title

**From Genomic Evidence to AI Decision Support: A Traceable Governance Framework for Multisource Biomedical Information — A Case Study of Borderline Personality Disorder**

## Research problem

AI-assisted biomedical systems increasingly interpret outputs produced by complex analytical pipelines rather than raw clinical observations. During this transformation, critical context can disappear:

- phenotype definition,
- data provenance,
- ancestry and population scope,
- statistical uncertainty,
- evidence type,
- causal status,
- limitations,
- source traceability.

A statistically correct source result may therefore become an unsupported or causally overstated AI statement.

## Case-study rationale

The BPD GWAS meta-analysis published in *Nature Genetics* (2026) is used as a high-complexity biomedical case.

The study combined:
- DSM-IV clinical samples,
- biobank/cohort samples largely defined using ICD codes,
- a self-reported diagnosis source,
- GWAS meta-analysis,
- gene-based analyses,
- polygenic scores (PGS),
- genetic correlations,
- PheWAS in BioVU and UK Biobank.

This heterogeneity makes the study useful for testing evidence-governance mechanisms.

## Research questions

### RQ1
How can heterogeneous genomic and clinical evidence be represented in a traceable information-governance framework for AI-assisted biomedical decision support?

### RQ2
Can provenance-aware governance reduce unsupported or causally overstated interpretations generated by AI systems?

### RQ3
Does explicit representation of population, phenotype, uncertainty, and causal boundaries improve the auditability of AI-generated biomedical interpretations?

## Hypotheses

- **H1:** Governance metadata increases evidence traceability.
- **H2:** Governance metadata reduces unsupported claims.
- **H3:** Explicit causal-boundary metadata reduces causal overreach.
- **H4:** Provenance-aware evidence representation increases auditability.
- **H5:** Population-scope metadata increases correct retention of generalizability boundaries.

## Method

The project follows a Design Science Research (DSR) structure:

1. **Problem identification** — evidence distortion in AI-assisted biomedical interpretation.
2. **Artifact design** — GenoTrace Evidence Unit + Governance Gate.
3. **Prototype implementation** — machine-readable evidence objects and validation rules.
4. **Experimental evaluation** — compare AI outputs under Conditions A, B, and C.
5. **Metric-based assessment** — evaluate traceability, unsupported claims, causal overreach, limitation retention, and population-boundary awareness.

## Unit of analysis

The primary unit of analysis is an **AI-generated biomedical claim**.

Each claim is evaluated against a structured **Evidence Unit** derived from the source publication.

## Experimental conditions

### Condition A — Raw evidence
Only the headline statistical result is provided.

Example:
```
BPD and PTSD genetic correlation: rg = 0.77.
Explain the finding.
```

### Condition B — Evidence + metadata
Adds method, confidence interval, source, and population.

### Condition C — Governance-aware evidence
Adds:
- evidence type,
- provenance,
- phenotype definition,
- population boundary,
- uncertainty,
- causal status,
- allowed interpretation,
- forbidden interpretation,
- source limitations.

## Expected contribution

The contribution is not a new genetic discovery about BPD.

The contribution is a reusable **biomedical evidence-governance layer** that can be applied to other domains such as oncology, cardiovascular genomics, clinical AI, and protein-design decision support.
